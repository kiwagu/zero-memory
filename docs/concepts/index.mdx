This section explains how zero-memory represents knowledge and why it is built
the way it is.

Start with the [memory model](/docs/concepts/memory-model): memories, kinds,
entities, links, and the MCP tools that operate on them. Stored content is
always canonical English.
[Canonical language](/docs/concepts/canonical-language) covers why, and how
other languages are handled on write, read, and in the dashboard.

Access is the next layer.
[Scopes and isolation](/docs/concepts/scopes-and-isolation) describes personal,
project, and team scopes, explicit membership, project-isolated reads, how a
session attaches to its project (and why an unattached write is refused), and
the verified portable layer. Writers have identities of their own.
[Agent principals](/docs/concepts/agent-principals) explains how non-human
writers are identified and what that identity enables across memories,
activity, and metrics, while [Identity](/docs/concepts/identity) covers
prefixed domain identifiers, branded id contracts, the system-of-record rule,
and how a project is identified.

The rest is lifecycle. [Provenance](/docs/concepts/provenance) records who
wrote a memory, how that ranks it, and which client it came from.
[Supersede and versioning](/docs/concepts/supersede-and-versioning) shows how
facts are updated without creating duplicates, and how version history forms
behind a current fact. [Open loops](/docs/concepts/open-loops) are tasks and
open questions that persist in briefings until closed.
