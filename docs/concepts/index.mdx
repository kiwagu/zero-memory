title: Concepts
description: The memory model and the design decisions behind zero-memory.
---

This section explains how zero-memory represents knowledge and why it is built
the way it is.

- [Memory model](/docs/concepts/memory-model) — memories, kinds, entities,
  links, and the MCP tools that operate on them.
- [Canonical language](/docs/concepts/canonical-language) — why stored
  content is canonical English, and how other languages are handled on write,
  read, and in the dashboard.
- [Scopes and isolation](/docs/concepts/scopes-and-isolation) — personal,
  project, and team scopes, explicit membership, project-isolated reads, how
  a session attaches to its project (and why an unattached write is
  refused), and the verified portable layer.
- [Agent principals](/docs/concepts/agent-principals) — how non-human writers
  are identified and what that identity enables across memories, activity,
  and metrics.
- [Identity](/docs/concepts/identity) — prefixed domain identifiers, branded
  id contracts, the system-of-record rule, and how a project is identified.
- [Provenance](/docs/concepts/provenance) — who wrote a memory, how that
  ranks it, and which client it came from.
- [Supersede and versioning](/docs/concepts/supersede-and-versioning) — how
  facts are updated without creating duplicates, and how version history
  forms behind a current fact.
- [Open loops](/docs/concepts/open-loops) — tasks and open questions that
  persist in briefings until closed.
